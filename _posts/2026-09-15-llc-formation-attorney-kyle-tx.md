## Do You Need an Attorney or Just a Filing Service?

You need an attorney if your LLC has multiple owners, holds real estate, involves outside investment, or operates in a licensed industry; a filing service is usually adequate for a simple single-member LLC with no partners and minimal liability exposure. The deciding factor is complexity, not business size.

Signs you should skip the template route:

- More than one owner splitting profits unevenly
- Plans to bring on investors or partners later
- Operating in construction, healthcare, or food service in Hays County
- Holding property or equipment inside the LLC

## What Should Be in Your Operating Agreement?

A Texas LLC operating agreement should define ownership percentages, profit distribution, management structure, and what happens if a member leaves or the business dissolves. Skipping this document — or using a generic template — is the single most common gap in new Kyle LLCs.

Core sections a proper operating agreement covers:

- Capital contributions and ownership percentages
- Voting rights and decision-making authority
- Profit and loss distribution method
- Buyout terms if a member exits or passes away
- Dissolution procedure

Without this document, Texas default LLC rules apply, which may not match what the owners actually agreed to verbally.

## What Ongoing Compliance Does a Kyle LLC Need?

After formation, a Kyle, TX LLC needs to maintain a registered agent, file an annual Texas Franchise Tax report (even if no tax is owed below the threshold), and keep the operating agreement updated as ownership changes. These aren't one-time tasks — they're recurring obligations that a formation-only attorney relationship doesn't cover.

| Ongoing Requirement | Frequency | Consequence of Missing It |
|---|---|---|
| Franchise tax report | Annual | Loss of good standing, penalties |
| Registered agent maintenance | Continuous | Missed legal notices, default judgments |
| Operating agreement updates | As needed | Disputes default to state law, not your terms |
| Business license renewals | Varies by industry | Fines, forced closure |

This is exactly where a flat-fee, one-time attorney relationship falls short — most solo formation attorneys don't include follow-up compliance work in their initial fee.
