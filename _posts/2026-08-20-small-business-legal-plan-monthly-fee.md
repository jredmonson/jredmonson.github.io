## Key Takeaways

- Small business legal plans typically start around $59.95/month, higher than personal plans, reflecting broader business-focused coverage.
- Core coverage generally includes contract and document review, debt collection letters, employment guidance, and compliance questions.
- The value case is usually about documents that would otherwise go unreviewed due to hourly-billing friction, not just responding to an active dispute.

Most small businesses run on contracts nobody ever had a lawyer actually read - a vendor agreement, a commercial lease, an independent contractor agreement signed because getting it reviewed the traditional way meant an hourly bill for something that might take twenty minutes. A small business legal plan is built specifically to remove that friction. As covered in the [full guide to LegalShield legal plans](https://jredmonson.github.io/law/legalshield-prepaid-legal-plans/), the flat monthly fee model that works for individuals scales to a business context with a coverage set built around what businesses actually run into.

## What a Business Plan Typically Costs

Small business legal plans generally start around $59.95 per month at the base tier, with Preferred and Premium tiers available above that for broader coverage and higher review limits. That's higher than a typical personal plan, which reflects both the broader scope of business legal needs and the higher stakes of getting a business document wrong. As with personal plans, exact current pricing varies and is worth [checking directly](https://lilliearnold.legalshieldassociate.com/) rather than assuming.

## What's Typically Covered

Business plans generally include contract and document review, debt collection letter assistance, employment matter guidance, and general business compliance questions - the recurring legal touchpoints a small business runs into far more often than an actual lawsuit. Some tiers add multi-state coverage options and the ability to designate multiple authorized users, which matters for a business with more than one owner or manager who needs legal access.

A commonly cited stat is worth noting here: small businesses that skip legal review on new contracts are meaningfully more likely to end up in a dispute over ambiguous terms than those that get agreements reviewed before signing - and reviewing after a dispute starts is a fundamentally more expensive position to be in than reviewing before signing. A flat-fee plan changes the incentive: since a review doesn't generate a separate bill, it's far more likely to actually happen before, not after, a contract gets signed.

## Where the Real Value Shows Up

The value of a plan like this rarely shows up as "we avoided one lawsuit" - it shows up as a quiet, ongoing habit change: contracts actually getting read before they're signed, a debt collection letter going out through the provider firm instead of getting written by the business owner alone, and a place to call with a quick employment question before it becomes a bigger issue. For a small business that currently has zero legal review built into its operations, that habit shift alone is often worth more than the monthly fee.

## Is It Worth It for a Small, Low-Volume Business?

Even a business with a relatively small number of contracts per year tends to get value from a plan like this, because the alternative - paying hourly for each individual review - discourages review altogether at low volume. If your business currently signs vendor agreements, leases, or contractor agreements without legal review simply because arranging it feels like too much friction for the size of the document, that's the exact gap this kind of plan closes. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a small business plan different from a personal LegalShield plan?**
Yes - business plans are priced and structured separately, with coverage built around business-specific needs like contract review, debt collection, and compliance rather than personal matters like wills.

**Can multiple people at my business use the plan?**
Some tiers allow designating multiple authorized users, which is worth confirming at signup if more than one owner or manager needs direct access.

**Does a business plan cover an employment dispute that's already active?**
Coverage is generally built around ongoing access from enrollment forward - an already-active dispute may fall outside what's covered, so it's worth confirming for your specific situation before assuming.
