## NAP Consistency: The Non-Negotiable Foundation

Every citation is only as good as its accuracy. If your business name, address, and phone number vary even slightly across listings — "St." vs "Street," a suite number present on one listing and missing on another — you create conflicting signals that can actually hurt more than help.

A proper build starts with a locked NAP standard before a single submission happens:

- Exact legal business name (matching your Google Business Profile)
- Full address formatted identically everywhere (abbreviations decided once, applied everywhere)
- One phone number used consistently — not a call-tracking number that changes
- Website URL with consistent protocol (https, no www vs www inconsistency)

The service should audit existing listings first. Canadian businesses that have been operating for years often have legacy listings with outdated addresses or old phone numbers scattered across directories nobody remembers submitting to. Cleaning these up matters as much as building new ones.

## Provincial and Municipal Layers That Add Local Weight

Canada's local search landscape has a provincial layer that doesn't exist the same way in most US state-level directory structures. Chambers of commerce in Canada tend to maintain more actively used business directories, and many cities maintain their own municipal business registries that get indexed and referenced.

A thorough build maps out:

- The specific province's chamber of commerce directory
- The specific municipality's business registry (if public-facing)
- Regional tourism boards (relevant for hospitality, retail, service businesses in tourist areas)
- Bilingual listing requirements where applicable (Quebec-specific directories often require French-language listings)

That last point trips up a lot of citation services. If you're building citations for a Quebec-based business, French-language directory submissions aren't optional extras — they're often the primary directories that matter in that market.

## Industry-Specific Canadian Associations

Beyond geographic directories, industry association memberships and directory listings carry specific weight for certain business categories. A Canadian citation service that understands the market will map your business to relevant sector directories rather than treating every business the same.

- Trades and home services: HomeStars, provincial trade licensing boards
- Healthcare: RateMDs, provincial college registries (where public-facing)
- Legal: Canadian Bar Association regional directories, Lawyer Referral Service listings
- Real estate: CREA-affiliated directories, provincial real estate board listings
- Restaurants/hospitality: OpenTable Canada, provincial tourism directories

This is where a generic citation package completely falls apart. There's no equivalent effort in a US-templated service to identify that a Vancouver-based electrician should be listed with the BC electrical licensing board's public directory, because that's not a directory pattern that exists identically in most US states.

## How to Evaluate a Citation Service Before You Buy

Before paying for any canada local business directory citation service, ask for specifics rather than accepting a vague "we submit to 50+ directories" pitch.

| Question to Ask | What a Good Answer Sounds Like |

| Do you handle provincial/municipal submissions? | Yes, with examples relevant to your specific province |
| How do you handle NAP audits before submission? | A documented audit process, not just "we check as we go" |

| Do you provide a report of exact URLs submitted? | Full URL list, not just a directory count |

If a service can't name specific Canadian directories beyond Google and Yelp, that's a signal they're running a generic template.
