## What a legitimate credit counseling agency actually does

A legitimate credit counseling service reviews your income, expenses, and debts, then builds a realistic budget and — if needed — a debt management plan that consolidates unsecured debt payments into one monthly amount at a negotiated lower interest rate. It does not erase debt, repair your credit score directly, or provide legal representation.

Here's what the process typically looks like:

| Step | What Happens | Typical Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Initial session | Budget review, debt analysis | $0-$75 |
| DMP setup (if recommended) | Enrollment in consolidated payment plan | $25-$75 one-time |
| Monthly maintenance | Ongoing plan management | $25-$50/month |
| Creditor negotiation | Lower interest rates, waived fees | Included in DMP fee |

Source: fee ranges reported by NFCC-member agency disclosures; actual costs vary by agency.

Agencies certified through the NFCC or FCAA are held to nonprofit standards and fee caps that for-profit debt settlement companies are not. If an agency in the Round Rock area is pushing you toward a lump-sum settlement instead of a monthly plan, or charging fees upfront before any counseling happens, that's a signal to walk away.

## Why Texas debt collection law matters even during counseling

Texas debt collection law protects you from harassment and false statements from collectors, and those protections apply whether or not you're enrolled in a counseling program. Credit counselors can advise you generally, but they can't cite the Texas Finance Code section that makes a specific collector call illegal or draft a cease-and-desist letter on your behalf.

Under the Texas Debt Collection Act and the federal FDCPA, collectors cannot:

- Call before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m.
- Threaten arrest or legal action they don't intend to take
- Contact your employer about the debt without permission
- Misrepresent the amount owed
- Continue contacting you in writing after you've requested they stop

If any of these happen while you're working with a credit counseling service in Round Rock, TX, that's a legal issue separate from your budget plan — and it's the kind of thing a legal plan membership is built to handle quickly, usually with a direct call to an attorney rather than researching it yourself at 9 p.m.

## What happens if a creditor actually sues you

If a creditor files a lawsuit against you in Williamson County, you generally have a limited number of days to file a formal answer, and missing that deadline can result in a default judgment against you regardless of whether you're in a debt management plan. Credit counselors are not attorneys and cannot file that answer or represent you in court.

This is the scenario most people don't plan for. They enroll in counseling, feel like the problem is handled, and then a summons shows up. A few things to know:

- A DMP payment history does not automatically stop a creditor from suing over a separate unpaid account.
- You typically must respond to a Texas lawsuit within a set window from the date you're served — check the summons for your specific deadline.
- Ignoring the summons is the single worst option; it almost always leads to a default judgment.

Having legal plan coverage in place before this happens means you have someone to call the same day, not after you've already missed a deadline.

## Nonprofit vs. for-profit: what actually differs

Nonprofit credit counseling agencies are typically funded partly by creditor contributions and charge modest, capped fees, while for-profit debt settlement companies charge a percentage of enrolled debt and often advise you to stop paying creditors entirely — a strategy that can trigger the exact lawsuits discussed above.

| Factor | Nonprofit Counseling | For-Profit Debt Settlement |
|---|---|---|
| Fee structure | Flat/capped monthly fee | Percentage of debt (often 15-25%) |
| Approach | Negotiate lower rates, keep paying | Stop paying, negotiate lump sum later |
| Lawsuit risk | Low (payments continue) | Higher (missed payments invite suits) |
| Credit impact | Moderate | Often more severe |
| Certification | NFCC/FCAA member | Often unregulated |

If an agency's model depends on you missing payments for months to build settlement leverage, understand that you're increasing your odds of being sued, which puts you back in legal-question territory.

## How to vet a credit counseling service before signing up

Before signing anything, confirm the agency is a licensed 501(c)(3) nonprofit, verify NFCC or FCAA membership, ask for a written fee schedule before your first session, and request references or reviews specific to Texas clients rather than national marketing claims.

A short checklist:

- Confirm nonprofit status and certification (NFCC.org or FCAA.org directories)
- Ask exactly what the monthly fee covers
- Get the fee schedule in writing before your session
- Ask how they handle creditors who won't agree to lower rates
- Ask directly: "What happens if I get sued while enrolled?" — a straight answer here tells you a lot

That last question matters more than most people realize. A good counselor will tell you plainly that they can't help with a lawsuit and will recommend you talk to an attorney. If they claim they can handle legal matters too, that's a red flag.

## FAQ

**Q: Is credit counseling free in Round Rock, TX?**
A: Many nonprofit agencies offer a free or low-cost initial consultation ($0-$75), but ongoing debt management plans typically carry a monthly fee of $25-$50 plus a small setup charge. Fully free services usually mean limited or generic advice rather than a customized plan.

**Q: Can a credit counselor stop debt collectors from calling me?**
A: A counselor can advise you on your rights and sometimes contact creditors on your behalf as part of a debt management plan, but they cannot legally enforce a stop to collector calls or represent you if a collector violates the law. That requires a legal remedy, not a budgeting one.

**Q: What's the difference between credit counseling and credit repair?**
A: Credit counseling focuses on budgeting and debt repayment plans; credit repair companies (often for-profit) claim to remove negative items from your credit report, which is a different and more regulated service under the Credit Repair Organizations Act.

**Q: Do I need a lawyer if I'm just behind on payments, not sued yet?**
A: Not necessarily for counseling itself, but having legal plan access before you're sued means you can get a quick answer the moment a collector oversteps or a summons arrives, rather than scrambling to find an attorney under a deadline.
