## Key Takeaways

- Higher-tier prepaid plans typically include uncontested divorce assistance, alongside general consultations on custody and family law questions.
- Contested divorces and active custody disputes generally require dedicated representation beyond what standard plan coverage includes.
- Getting guidance early - before a situation becomes contested - is where a plan's coverage tends to provide the most real value.

Divorce and custody questions rarely start as emergencies - they usually start as uncertainty: whether to file, what a fair custody arrangement looks like, what happens to shared property. As covered in the [comparison guide to whether prepaid legal is worth it](https://jredmonson.github.io/law/is-prepaid-legal-worth-it-comparison/), this is exactly the kind of common, recurring legal need a plan is built to support well.

## What's Typically Covered

Consultations on custody and divorce questions are generally included at the base tier of most personal plans, giving members somewhere to ask early questions before deciding how to proceed. Higher tiers - typically Advanced and above - often add uncontested divorce assistance specifically, covering situations where both parties already agree on the terms and the process is primarily about paperwork and formalizing the agreement.

## Where Coverage Typically Stops

A contested divorce - where custody, support, or property division is actively disputed - generally falls outside standard plan coverage and calls for dedicated representation from an attorney focused specifically on that case. The same is usually true for a custody dispute already active in court before plan enrollment began. This boundary matters because it's the difference between a plan handling a situation well and a plan simply not being built for it.

## Where the Real Value Shows Up

The clearest value from plan coverage in this area tends to come early - getting a straightforward answer to "what happens if..." before a decision is made, or getting an uncontested divorce processed without the cost of hiring a private attorney for something both parties already agree on. Family law questions asked early, before positions harden into a dispute, are exactly what consultation coverage is designed to help with.

## Is This Coverage Enough for Your Situation?

If the situation is still at the question-asking stage, or is genuinely uncontested, plan coverage is likely to serve it well. If it's already contested or headed toward litigation, dedicated representation is the more appropriate path, and it's worth having that conversation directly with a family law attorney. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does an uncontested divorce still require a court appearance?**
Requirements vary by state - a provider firm assisting with an uncontested divorce can typically walk through what's required in your specific state.

**Can I get help with a custody question without filing for divorce?**
Yes - custody-related consultations are generally available as part of standard coverage, separate from whether a divorce filing is involved.

**What happens if an uncontested divorce becomes contested partway through?**
This is exactly the kind of situation worth discussing directly with the provider firm early, since coverage boundaries can shift once a matter becomes contested.
