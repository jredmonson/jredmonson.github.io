## Step 1: Lock the Niche Before You Touch Hostinger

Don't open the Hostinger dashboard until you've written down your niche, your primary affiliate program, and three sub-topics you'll cover in the first month. This isn't busywork — it's the single biggest time-saver in the entire process, because every decision after this point (page names, menu structure, content topics) flows from it.

A vague niche like "make money online" produces vague pages and stalls momentum, because every piece of content feels like it could go five different directions. A specific niche — "budget-friendly VPN reviews for remote workers" — tells you exactly what your homepage says, what your first three posts should be, and which affiliate programs to prioritize.

- Niche: one sentence, specific enough that you could name 10 article titles right now
- Primary affiliate program: the one you'll feature on the homepage
- Secondary programs: 2-3 backups for content variety
- Audience: who specifically has the problem you're solving

If you can't fill in all four bullets in under ten minutes, the niche isn't ready. Fix that before touching hosting.

## Step 3: Choosing Between WordPress and Hostinger Horizons

Hostinger gives you two realistic paths for an affiliate site: traditional WordPress with a theme, or Hostinger Horizons, which uses AI prompts to generate site structure and content scaffolding. Which one you pick affects your one-day timeline more than almost any other decision.

WordPress gives you full control and the largest plugin ecosystem, which matters if you're planning comparison tables, review schema, or complex affiliate link management down the line. The tradeoff is setup time — you're choosing a theme, configuring menus, and installing plugins manually.

Hostinger Horizons compresses that by letting you describe the site you want in a prompt and generating a working structure from it. For a first affiliate site, especially one built in a single day, this removes a lot of the theme-selection paralysis that eats hours.

- **Choose WordPress if:** you want long-term plugin flexibility, already know WordPress, or plan heavy content customization
- **Choose Hostinger Horizons if:** this is your first site, you want speed over customization, or you're testing a niche before committing resources
- **Either way:** you can migrate structure later — the choice isn't permanent, just a starting point

## Step 4: Building the Three Pages That Actually Matter

You don't need twenty pages on day one. You need three: a homepage that states what the site is about, an about/disclosure page (required for affiliate compliance), and one pillar content page tied to your primary affiliate offer.

The homepage doesn't need to be clever. It needs to say what the site covers, who it's for, and where to go next — usually a link into your pillar page or top category. Overthinking homepage copy is one of the most common one-day-build killers; a plain, clear homepage beats a polished one that took four hours.

| Page | Purpose | Minimum Content |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Orient the visitor | 150-300 words, clear nav, 1 CTA |
| About/Disclosure | Compliance + trust | FTC affiliate disclosure, short bio |
| Pillar page | Primary conversion page | 800-1,200 words, comparison or review format |

The disclosure page is not optional. If you're linking to affiliate offers — including Hostinger's own affiliate program — you need a clear, visible disclosure statement. This isn't a nice-to-have; it's a compliance requirement in most jurisdictions, including under FTC guidelines in the U.S.

## Step 5: Writing Your First Three Posts

A site with three pages and no posts looks unfinished to both visitors and search engines. Before you call the one-day build done, you need three posts that support your pillar page and give the site some initial content depth.

The fastest path is picking posts that answer specific questions your target audience is already searching for, rather than broad topic overviews. "Best budget VPNs for remote workers" is a better first post than "Everything About VPNs," because it's scoped tightly enough to write in one sitting and it maps directly to a buying decision.

- Post 1: A direct comparison or "best of" list tied to your primary affiliate program
- Post 2: A problem/solution post addressing a specific pain point your audience searches for
- Post 3: A "how to" post that naturally links back to your pillar page and disclosure

Each post should be 800-1,500 words, include one clear affiliate CTA, and link internally to your pillar page. Don't aim for perfection here — aim for published. You can revise content after the site is live; you can't revise a site that doesn't exist yet.

## Step 6: What to Skip on Day One

Part of finishing in a day is knowing what NOT to build yet. Email opt-ins, advanced plugin stacks, custom design work, and a full content calendar are all things that can wait until week two. Trying to do all of it on launch day is the most common reason "one day" turns into "one month."

Skip anything that doesn't directly affect whether a visitor can read your content and click an affiliate link today. Analytics setup, social sharing buttons, and comment systems are useful but not launch-blocking — add them once the core site is live and generating your first bit of traffic.

| Skip on Day 1 | Add Later |
|---|---|
| Email list / opt-in forms | Week 2, once you have visitors |
| Custom logo/branding | After validating the niche |
| Full content calendar | After first 3 posts get traction |
| Advanced SEO plugins | Once basic pages are indexed |
