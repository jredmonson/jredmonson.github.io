## Rural Property Brings Considerations a Standard Suburban Closing Doesn't

Buying or selling acreage in Bastrop County often involves questions that don't come up in a standard suburban transaction: easements for shared driveways or utility access, mineral rights that may or may not transfer with the surface property, and well and septic systems that require their own inspections and disclosures separate from a standard home inspection. A title company handling a routine suburban closing isn't necessarily equipped to flag these issues - which is exactly where a real estate attorney's review becomes valuable specifically for rural Bastrop County transactions.

Mineral rights in particular are worth explicit attention: Texas law allows surface and mineral ownership to be separated, meaning a property's previous owner (or their heirs) may still hold mineral rights even after selling the surface land - a detail that a standard contract can gloss over if it isn't specifically addressed.
