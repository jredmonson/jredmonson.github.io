Most affiliate marketers treat text link ads like a clearance bin — grab whatever's cheapest, slap in a link, wait for clicks. That's backwards. Text links are cheap because they're a low-friction ad unit, not because they're low-value. The format has been underpriced relative to its potential since forum sig links and blogroll placements were the norm, and that gap hasn't closed even with newer marketplaces.

The mistake isn't buying text link traffic. It's buying it the same way you'd buy a lottery ticket — cheapest option, no vetting, hope for the best. If you're asking what's the cheapest way to buy text link ads traffic that still converts, the honest answer is that "cheapest" and "converts" are two separate variables you have to solve independently. Price gets you volume. Relevance gets you conversions. You need both, but you can't shortcut the second one by spending more on the first.

This piece breaks down where text link ads actually get cheap, how to tell a cheap-but-dead placement from a cheap-and-live one, and what a realistic testing budget looks like if you're running this on an affiliate marketer's budget, not an agency retainer.

## Why Text Link Ads Are the Cheapest Format to Begin With

Text links don't require creative production, don't compete for above-the-fold banner real estate, and don't trigger the same ad-blindness reflex a flashy banner does. That combination keeps their price floor lower across nearly every ad network and marketplace.

Advertisers also underbid on text links because they underestimate them. Most media buyers chase impressions and visual impact, so text link inventory goes unsold or gets priced as an afterthought. That's the opening for anyone buying with intent instead of habit.

| Ad Format | Typical Cost Structure | Why It's Priced This Way |
|---|---|---|
| Text link ads | Flat rate or low CPM | No creative, low visual competition, underbid by most buyers |
| Banner ads | Higher CPM | Requires design, more screen real estate |
| Native ads | CPC, often higher | Platform fees, algorithmic placement, higher demand |
| Pop-unders | Very low CPM | High volume, notoriously low intent |

## The Real Cost Variable: Relevance, Not Price

Here's the practitioner point of view that gets skipped in most "cheap traffic" guides: the format doesn't convert, the audience does. A text link on a page about home fitness equipment will outperform a banner on an unrelated general traffic site, even if the banner costs three times as much per click.

When you're evaluating cheap text link inventory, ask what the page is about, not what network it's sold through. A $0.03 click from a page whose readers are actively researching your exact niche beats a $0.01 click from a random content farm every time. This is where affiliate marketers waste money — they compare unit price without comparing audience intent.

- Check if the linking page ranks or gets traffic for terms related to your offer.
- Look at the other ads or affiliate links already on that page — if competitors are buying there repeatedly, that's a signal.
- Avoid placements on pages with no clear topical focus, even if the price is rock-bottom.

## How to Test Without Wasting Your Budget

Don't buy one big placement and hope. Split a small budget — $20 to $50 — across three to five different text link placements in your niche. Track clicks and conversions separately for each. This tells you which specific pages actually send buyers, not just visitors.

The goal of this first round isn't profit. It's data. You're identifying which 1-2 placements out of five are worth scaling. Once you find a winner, you can negotiate a longer-term rate or recurring placement, which usually drops your per-click cost further.

- Round 1: Small test buys across multiple relevant pages.
- Round 2: Kill anything with zero conversions after a reasonable click threshold.
- Round 3: Scale spend only on placements with proven conversion activity.

## Common Mistakes That Make "Cheap" Traffic Worthless

The most common failure is buying based on network reputation instead of individual page quality. A network can have great pages and terrible ones in the same inventory pool — averaging "the network is good" across every purchase is a mistake.

Another failure: sending text link traffic to a generic homepage instead of a landing page that matches the context the reader was just in. Someone clicking a text link about budget kitchen gadgets doesn't want to land on your all-purpose affiliate hub. Match the landing experience to the click's context.

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Buying by network reputation only | Mixed-quality placements, wasted spend |
| Sending clicks to a generic homepage | Higher bounce, lower conversion |
| Ignoring click-to-conversion tracking | No way to know which placement to scale |
| One large buy with no test phase | Can't isolate what worked or failed |

## Setting Realistic Expectations for ROI

Text link ads won't outperform highly targeted paid search or retargeting on a per-click basis in most cases — that's not the point. The point is cost efficiency at volume, especially for affiliate offers with a lower price point or recurring commission structure where you need cheap clicks to hit profitable scale.

Expect variance. Some placements will underperform even with good vetting, because traffic quality shifts over time. Revisit placements quarterly rather than assuming a good result today holds for the next six months.

## Scaling Once You Find What Works

Once a placement proves itself, negotiate directly if the marketplace allows it, or lock in a longer-term rate. Recurring placements on proven pages are where the real cost efficiency shows up — you stop paying for the discovery phase and just pay for delivery.

Reinvest saved cost from underperforming placements into your winners. This is basic budget discipline, but it's the step most affiliate marketers skip because they're chasing new cheap inventory instead of doubling down on what's already working.
