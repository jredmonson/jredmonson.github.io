## What a Part-Time Receptionist Actually Costs

A part-time receptionist working 15-25 hours a week typically costs several hundred to over a thousand dollars a month in wages alone, before payroll taxes, training time, sick days, and eventual turnover and rehiring costs are factored in. That figure covers only the hours they're scheduled, not the other 143-153 hours in a week when the phone still rings.

The real cost runs higher than the hourly wage suggests. Payroll taxes typically add another 7-10% on top of wages. Training a new hire to competently handle calls - learning the business's services, its booking system, and its team's preferences - takes real time before they're fully productive, and that ramp-up period is a sunk cost every time turnover happens. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics has consistently reported average annual turnover in front-desk and administrative roles well above 40%, meaning most local businesses running a part-time receptionist model are re-training a replacement more often than they'd like.

## What an AI Voice Receptionist Actually Costs

An AI voice receptionist runs as a flat monthly service cost that is typically a fraction of even a single part-time employee's wages, and it covers every hour of every day rather than a scheduled shift. There's no payroll tax, no sick leave, no turnover, and no ramp-up period after the initial setup and training phase.

The setup itself is a one-time investment - training the AI on the business's services, hours, and routing rules - after which the monthly cost stays flat regardless of call volume spikes, holiday coverage, or after-hours demand. This is the core economic difference: a part-time hire's cost is tied to hours scheduled, while an AI voice receptionist's cost is tied to a flat service fee that doesn't change whether it answers ten calls or two hundred in a given week.

## Hours of Coverage: The Real Gap

The starkest difference between the two options isn't the dollar cost - it's the hours of coverage each one actually provides. A part-time receptionist covers roughly 9-15% of the hours in a week; an AI voice receptionist covers 100% of them, including nights, weekends, and holidays when call volume for service businesses often spikes.

For a plumbing or HVAC company, the hours outside a typical 9-to-5 shift are exactly when emergency calls are most likely to come in - a burst pipe or a failed AC unit doesn't wait for business hours. A part-time receptionist scheduled for daytime hours simply isn't there for those calls, which is why so many of them go to voicemail and, per widely-cited call-tracking data, a majority of those callers never leave a message at all. An AI voice receptionist closes that exact gap without needing a night-shift hire, which most small local businesses can't justify staffing anyway.

## Simultaneous Calls: A Gap Wages Can't Fix

A human receptionist, no matter how skilled, can only handle one phone call at a time - every additional simultaneous caller goes to voicemail or a busy signal. An AI voice receptionist can handle multiple calls at once, so a busy Monday morning with three calls arriving within the same minute results in three answered, qualified calls instead of one answered and two missed.

This matters more than it might seem for businesses with any real call volume, since the busiest call windows for most local businesses - right after opening, during lunch, and right before closing - are exactly when a single front-desk person is most likely to already be on another line. No amount of additional part-time hours fixes this without hiring a second person to cover the same shift, which pushes the cost comparison even further in the AI receptionist's favor.

## Where a Part-Time Hire Still Makes Sense

A part-time receptionist brings in-person presence, the ability to greet walk-in customers, and general office tasks beyond phone answering that an AI voice receptionist isn't built to do. For businesses with meaningful walk-in traffic or that need someone physically present to manage the front office, a part-time hire still fills a real role an AI receptionist doesn't replace.

The two aren't mutually exclusive, and most local businesses that adopt an AI voice receptionist aren't eliminating existing staff - they're covering the hours and overflow calls that staff physically can't reach. A dental office keeps its front-desk person for in-person check-in and insurance conversations while the AI receptionist covers after-hours booking requests and calls that come in while the front desk is helping a patient checking out. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does an AI voice receptionist replace the need for any staff at all?**
Not typically - it replaces the task of answering every incoming call, but most businesses still keep staff for in-person duties, complex customer service, and the calls that genuinely need a human's judgment.

**Is the cost comparison different for a business with very low call volume?**
The relative cost advantage narrows somewhat, but the hours-of-coverage advantage doesn't change - even a low-call-volume business still benefits from never missing the calls that do come in outside business hours.

**Can a business start with an AI voice receptionist and add part-time staff later, or the reverse?**
Yes - the two aren't a permanent either-or decision, and many businesses adjust the mix as call volume and staffing needs change over time.
