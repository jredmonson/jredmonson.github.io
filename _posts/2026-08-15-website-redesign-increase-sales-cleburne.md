## When a Full Redesign Is Actually Warranted

A full redesign makes sense when a site has genuinely structural problems: confusing navigation that hides key information, a design that doesn't work properly on mobile devices, or load times slow enough to cause visitors to leave before the page finishes loading. These are foundational issues that targeted fixes can't resolve.

## When a Smaller Fix Is the Better Call

If the site's structure is basically sound but sales are still flat, the more likely culprits are a missing or unclear call-to-action, copy that talks about the business instead of the customer's problem, or - very commonly - no conversion tracking at all, meaning nobody actually knows which pages or traffic sources are underperforming. In Cleburne's lighter-competition market, these targeted fixes often move the needle faster and cheaper than a full rebuild.

## How to Tell Which Situation You're In

A simple diagnostic: pull up the site on a phone and try to find the phone number or contact form within five seconds. If that's difficult, that's a real structural issue worth a redesign. If it's easy to find but sales still aren't happening, the problem is more likely persuasion or tracking - a smaller, less expensive fix.

## A Simple Framework for Deciding: Redesign or Repair

Before committing to a full redesign, a Cleburne business owner can run a quick internal test: list the three most common objections or questions customers have before buying, and check whether the current website actually answers each one clearly within the first screen a visitor sees. If the answers are buried or missing, that's often fixable with targeted copy and layout changes rather than requiring an entirely new site built from scratch.

## Why Cost Matters More in a Market This Size

Given Cleburne's more modest local search competition compared to Waco, the return on a full expensive redesign is often lower here than the same investment would produce in a bigger, more competitive market - which makes the smaller, targeted-fix path even more attractive for most Johnson County small businesses evaluating their options.

## What Tracking Reveals Before Any Redesign Decision

Before spending on either a full redesign or a smaller fix, installing proper analytics and conversion tracking for even 30 days often reveals exactly where visitors are dropping off - a specific page, a particular device type, or a certain traffic source - turning a vague "sales are flat" concern into a concrete, fixable problem.

**Is a mobile-only redesign ever a reasonable middle option?**
Yes - if desktop performance is fine but mobile conversion is clearly weaker (a common pattern), focusing redesign effort specifically on mobile can be more cost-effective than a full site rebuild.

**How long should a Cleburne business wait to judge whether a fix worked?**
At least 30 days of consistent traffic is usually needed to judge a conversion-focused change fairly, since day-to-day fluctuation can otherwise make a good fix look inconclusive too early.
