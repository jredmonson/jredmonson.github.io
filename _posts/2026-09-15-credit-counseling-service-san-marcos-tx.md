## Why Do Students in San Marcos Need a Different Approach?

Texas State students typically carry student loan debt and small revolving credit card balances rather than mortgages or auto loans, which means their counseling priority is usually building credit history safely, not digging out of collections. A student's first move should be budgeting help and a plan to avoid missed payments, not a full DMP enrollment.

Common student debt situations in San Marcos:

- First credit card opened at 18-19 with a low limit, maxed out within a semester
- Federal or private student loans in deferment, unclear about repayment timelines
- Auto loan co-signed by a parent, student unaware of the credit impact
- Rental agreements with security deposit disputes that don't involve debt at all but feel financially urgent

That last item is worth flagging because it's not a credit issue at all, it's a legal one. A landlord withholding a deposit, or a lease dispute near campus, isn't something a credit counselor handles. That's a contract question, and it's the kind of thing a legal plan membership is actually built for.

## When Does a Debt Problem Become a Legal Problem?

A debt problem becomes a legal problem the moment a creditor files a lawsuit, threatens wage garnishment, or a collector violates the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act by harassing you outside legal hours or misrepresenting the debt. At that point, credit counseling alone won't protect you.

Signs you've crossed that line:

1. You've received a court summons related to unpaid debt
2. A collector is calling before 8am or after 9pm, or contacting your employer
3. You're being threatened with wage garnishment without a court order
4. A debt has been reported that isn't yours (identity theft or reporting error)

Credit counseling agencies can help you understand your budget and negotiate payment terms voluntarily, but they generally can't send a legal letter demanding a collector stop contact, and they can't represent you in court. That's the gap a legal plan fills, and it's worth understanding the cost difference: a single consultation with a Hays County consumer protection attorney can run $200-$400 an hour, while a legal plan membership covers ongoing access for a flat monthly rate.

## What Should You Ask a San Marcos Credit Counseling Agency Before Signing Up?

You should ask for a full written fee schedule, confirmation of NFCC or FCAA accreditation, and a clear explanation of how the agency is compensated by creditors before agreeing to any debt management plan. If an agency can't answer these plainly, that's a red flag.

Questions to ask directly:

- What is your total fee structure, setup and monthly, in writing?
- Are you accredited by the NFCC or Financial Counseling Association of America?
- Do creditors pay you a percentage of what I repay (fair share)?
- What happens if I miss a payment on the debt management plan?
- Can you help if I'm already facing a lawsuit or wage garnishment?

That last question usually gets a "no, we recommend you speak to an attorney," which is honest and correct, it's just not the answer people expect when they first search for help.
