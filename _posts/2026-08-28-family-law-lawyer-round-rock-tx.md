## Why Round Rock Divorces Often Involve More Complex Finances

When both spouses work in Round Rock's corporate and tech sector, a divorce case often has to account for stock options, RSUs, employer stock purchase plans, and 401(k) balances that are harder to divide cleanly than a simple bank account. Texas is a community property state, which generally means property and income acquired during the marriage is treated as jointly owned regardless of whose name is on the account - a rule that surprises some people the first time it comes up in their own case.

Getting an early, accurate picture of what actually counts as community property versus separate property (assets owned before the marriage, or received individually as a gift or inheritance) is one of the most valuable things a family law consultation does upfront, before either side starts making assumptions about what they're entitled to.

## Williamson County Family Court: What to Expect in Round Rock

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Texas a community property state?**
**A:** Yes - Texas generally treats property and income acquired during a marriage as community property owned jointly by both spouses, regardless of whose name is on the account, with some exceptions for separate property owned before the marriage or received individually as a gift or inheritance.

**Q: How are stock options or RSUs divided in a Round Rock divorce?**
**A:** Equity compensation earned during the marriage is generally treated as community property subject to division, though valuing unvested awards can be complex - this is an area where working with an attorney familiar with tech-sector compensation matters.

**Q: How long does a contested divorce take in Williamson County?**
**A:** Timelines vary based on the court's caseload and how contested the case is, but Williamson County's growth has generally increased wait times for hearing dates compared to previous years - an attorney working in the county regularly can give a more current estimate.
