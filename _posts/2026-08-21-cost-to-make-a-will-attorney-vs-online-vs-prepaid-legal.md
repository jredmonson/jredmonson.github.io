## Key Takeaways

- Attorney-drafted wills typically run $300-$600 for a simple individual estate and $1,000-$2,500 for a couple with children or multiple properties.
- Online DIY will services are cheaper upfront ($100-$500) but carry real risk - an error or omission can invalidate the document entirely.
- A prepaid legal plan sits between these two options: real attorney involvement through a provider law firm, included as a standard membership benefit rather than billed separately.

Cost is one of the most commonly cited reasons people delay getting a will done, and the honest answer is that it depends heavily on which path is used. As covered in the [Make-A-Will Month guide](https://jredmonson.github.io/law/national-make-a-will-month-guide/), understanding these three paths clearly is the fastest way to stop putting this off.

## Option 1: A Traditional Attorney

A simple will through an attorney for a single individual with few assets typically costs $300-$600. For a couple with minor children or multiple properties, that range moves to roughly $1,000-$2,500, and more complex estates - multiple properties, business interests, blended families - can run $5,000 or more. The advantage here is direct, personalized attention from an attorney who can address complexity a template can't, at a cost that reflects that complexity.

## Option 2: Online DIY Services

Online will services generally run $100-$500, using a guided questionnaire to generate a state-compliant document without direct attorney involvement. The upfront savings are real, but so is the risk: an error or omission in a self-drafted document can invalidate all or part of it, and that failure is typically discovered only after death, when it's too late to fix - exactly the moment a will exists to prevent complications, not create new ones.

## Option 3: A Prepaid Legal Plan

Will preparation is included as a standard benefit on most personal-tier prepaid legal plans, generally in the $30-$60/month range for the overall membership - meaning the will itself doesn't generate a separate bill on top of the monthly cost. This option combines real attorney involvement, through an assigned provider law firm, with the cost structure of a flat membership rather than a one-time attorney bill. For anyone who wants attorney-level involvement without the attorney-level upfront price tag, this is the middle path between the other two options.

## Which Option Actually Makes Sense

The right choice depends on complexity and what's already being paid for elsewhere. A very simple estate with no dependents might be well-served by a careful online service. A complex estate with business interests or a blended family likely benefits from a dedicated attorney's full attention. And anyone who already has - or is considering - a prepaid legal plan for other reasons gets will preparation essentially bundled in, which is often the most cost-efficient path when the underlying membership is already worth it for its other benefits. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is an online will actually legally valid?**
Generally yes, if completed correctly and properly executed according to state requirements (witnesses, notarization where required) - the risk is in errors or omissions during completion, not the format itself.

**Does a prepaid legal plan's will benefit cover updates later?**
Higher tiers commonly include annual will updates - worth confirming at the specific tier level since this varies by plan.

**Is the cheapest option always the DIY option?**
Not necessarily once ongoing costs are considered - a prepaid legal plan's monthly fee often works out comparable to or less than a one-time attorney fee over a year, while including attorney involvement DIY services don't offer.
