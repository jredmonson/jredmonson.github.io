## What documents will an estate planning attorney in Smithville TX prepare for me?
A qualified estate planning attorney will prepare a tailored package that usually includes a will, a durable power of attorney for finances, a medical directive or advance health care directive, and HIPAA release forms. They may also prepare a revocable living trust if you want to avoid probate for certain assets.

Key documents and short descriptions:
- Last Will & Testament: names executor and guardians; must be witnessed under Texas rules (Texas requires two competent witnesses).  
- Durable Power of Attorney (financial): allows your chosen agent to manage money immediately or upon incapacity.  
- Medical Power of Attorney / Advance Directive: names a surrogate for healthcare decisions and often includes a HIPAA release.  
- Revocable Living Trust: can hold assets to avoid probate for those assets; funding a trust typically requires retitling accounts.  
- Self-proving affidavit: signed with a notary to simplify probate by avoiding witness testimony.

Concrete notes:
- Texas law requires two witnesses to sign a will for it to be valid.  
- You should sign a self-proving will in front of a notary to speed probate procedures if probate becomes necessary.

## How long does an estate plan take to create and how long does probate take in Smithville TX?
A basic estate plan can be completed in days to a few weeks; complex plans with trusts and asset retitling take longer. Probate in Texas, when required, typically takes at least 6–12 months in uncontested cases and longer if disputes arise.

Typical timeframes:
- Initial consultation: 60–90 minutes.  
- Drafting a simple will and powers of attorney: often 1–2 weeks after the initial meeting, depending on client responsiveness.  
- Creating a trust-based plan and funding accounts: commonly 2–6 weeks, sometimes longer if title transfers are needed.  
- Probate: at minimum 6–12 months for straightforward estates; contested or complex estates can take a year or more.

## What should I ask during a consultation with a Smithville TX estate planning attorney?
Ask direct questions about experience, fees, process, and ongoing support so you can compare firms meaningfully. Focus on how the attorney will solve your particular goals: protecting a surviving spouse, planning for minor children, or minimizing probate.

Essential consultation questions:
- How many years have you practiced estate planning and how many similar matters have you handled?  
- Do you charge a flat fee for a will package or an hourly rate; what precisely is included?  
- Will you draft the documents personally or delegate to staff/associates?  
- How long will it take to finalize documents after I provide information?  
- Do you assist with funding trusts and transferring titles? Is that included in the fee?  
- How do you handle updates, and what is the cost to revise documents in the future?

Checklist to bring to the meeting:
- List of assets (real estate, bank accounts, retirement accounts, life insurance).  
- Beneficiary designations for retirement and life insurance.  
- Names and contact info for chosen executors, trustees, and guardians.  
- Questions about taxes, Medicaid planning, or business succession if relevant.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### How much does estate planning cost in Smithville TX?
Costs vary by the complexity of your situation and the attorney’s billing model. Many attorneys offer flat-fee packages for simple wills and powers of attorney, while trust-based plans or complex tax planning are typically higher and may be quoted as a flat fee or hourly rate. Ask for a written fee estimate.

### How long does it take to set up an estate plan?
The process can take from a few days (for simple, already-prepared documents) to several weeks for drafted and reviewed documents. Expect a 60–90 minute initial consultation, then allow 1–6 weeks depending on complexity and whether trust funding or property retitling is required.

### Is hiring an estate planning attorney worth it for a small estate?
Yes, an attorney helps ensure documents are valid under Texas law, names correct beneficiaries, handles guardianship questions, and reduces the chance of costly probate mistakes. For small, straightforward estates, a simple will and powers of attorney may suffice, but legal advice reduces future disputes and administrative burden.

### What happens if I die without a will in Texas?
If you die intestate (without a will), Texas intestacy rules determine who inherits based on relationship—typically a spouse and children or other relatives—rather than your personal choices. Intestate estates usually still go through probate, which can be time-consuming and may not match your wishes.
