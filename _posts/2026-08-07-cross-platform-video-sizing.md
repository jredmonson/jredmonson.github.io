## Key Takeaways

- YouTube long-form wants 16:9 landscape (1920x1080); YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram Reels want 9:16 vertical (1080x1920).
- Instagram feed posts perform better at 4:5 or 1:1 square than at full vertical 9:16.
- Every vertical platform reserves 15-20% of the top and bottom of the frame for UI elements - captions, usernames, and buttons will cover anything placed there.
- Re-cropping instead of re-exporting is the fastest way to kill a video's watch time on the platform it wasn't built for.
- Local business owners without in-house editing capacity typically outsource this to keep content consistent across channels without burning hours per post.

## Introduction

Cross-platform video sizing is the difference between content that looks native to a feed and content that looks like it was dumped there because someone had five minutes to spare. Every platform built its player around a specific aspect ratio and a specific set of safe zones, and ignoring that isn't a small aesthetic miss - it's a signal to both viewers and the algorithm that the content wasn't made for them.

Most small business accounts and solo marketers skip this step because reformatting for three or four platforms feels like triple the work for one video. That's a reasonable objection right up until you watch a TikTok-native vertical clip perform fine on TikTok and then completely bomb when the same file gets uploaded raw to a YouTube Short with black bars on both sides. The algorithm doesn't know the content is good - it only knows the video isn't filling the frame, and it treats that as a weak signal.

## What Aspect Ratio Should Each Platform Use

Each major platform has a dominant aspect ratio, and posting outside it forces the player to add black bars or crop the frame in ways you didn't control. Matching the native ratio is the single highest-leverage formatting decision you'll make before a video goes live.

| Platform | Format | Aspect Ratio | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube (long-form) | Landscape | 16:9 | 1920x1080 |
| YouTube Shorts | Vertical | 9:16 | 1080x1920 |
| TikTok | Vertical | 9:16 | 1080x1920 |
| Instagram Reels | Vertical | 9:16 | 1080x1920 |
| Instagram Feed | Square/Portrait | 1:1 or 4:5 | 1080x1080 or 1080x1350 |
| Facebook Feed | Square/Landscape | 1:1 or 16:9 | 1080x1080 or 1920x1080 |
| LinkedIn | Square/Landscape | 1:1 or 16:9 | 1080x1080 or 1920x1080 |

Notice that Instagram feed posts aren't 9:16 even though Reels are - feed placement historically rewards content that isn't full vertical, since it lets more of the grid show in a scroll.

## How Do Safe Zones Differ Between TikTok and Instagram

Safe zones differ because each platform places its UI elements - captions, usernames, like/share buttons, sound titles - in different spots on the frame, and anything placed there gets covered no matter how good the edit is. TikTok's UI sits lower and more toward the right edge; Instagram's Reels UI runs the full right side plus a caption band near the bottom.

- **TikTok safe zone**: keep key text out of the bottom 130px and the right-side 80px (username, follow button, share icons stack vertically on the right).
- **Instagram Reels safe zone**: keep text out of the bottom 250px (caption bar sits higher than TikTok's) and the right-side 100px.
- **YouTube Shorts safe zone**: similar to Reels but the subscribe button and channel name sit slightly higher - leave the bottom 200px clear.
- **General rule**: center-frame text and subject placement survives every platform's UI; anything you need visible should sit in the middle 60% of the frame both horizontally and vertically.

## Should You Re-Export or Re-Crop for Each Platform

Re-export from the original edit whenever possible instead of cropping the already-exported file, because cropping a finished 16:9 export down to 9:16 throws away resolution and often cuts off subjects that were framed for a wider shot. A proper multi-format workflow shoots or frames with the tightest ratio in mind from the start.

1. Shoot in a ratio wide enough to crop down (shoot 16:9 or higher, never shoot square and try to expand).
2. Frame subjects centered so a 9:16 crop from a 16:9 source doesn't cut off faces or on-screen text.
3. Export the landscape master first for YouTube.
4. Re-crop from the master timeline (not the exported file) for each vertical platform to preserve resolution.
5. Adjust caption placement per platform's safe zone before final render - don't reuse the same caption position file across all four exports.

## What Resolution and Bitrate Actually Matter

Resolution and bitrate matter because platforms recompress every upload, and starting with a file that's already too low-quality means the platform's compression makes it worse, while starting too high wastes upload time without a visible benefit. Match the target spec instead of guessing.

| Platform | Minimum Resolution | Recommended Bitrate |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 1920x1080 | 8-12 Mbps (1080p) |
| TikTok | 1080x1920 | 5-8 Mbps |
| Instagram Reels | 1080x1920 | 5-8 Mbps |
| Facebook | 1080x1080 or 1920x1080 | 4-8 Mbps |

Uploading a 4K master when the platform caps playback at 1080p doesn't hurt quality, but it does slow down upload and processing time for no visible gain on mobile screens, which is where most of this content gets watched anyway.

## How Should Captions and Text Overlays Change Per Platform

Captions and text overlays need different vertical positioning per platform because each one reserves a different amount of bottom-frame space for its own UI - a caption placed at the bottom third on TikTok will sit fine there but get half-covered by Instagram's caption bar and username stack.

- Position captions in the center-third of the vertical frame, not the bottom third, when exporting for multi-platform use.
- Use larger font sizes (60-80px minimum at 1080 width) since mobile viewing distance and screen size vary widely.
- Avoid platform-specific auto-caption tools for the final export if reposting elsewhere - each tool burns in its own font and position, which then has to be covered or re-edited for other platforms.
- Keep a single caption style ready to reposition per platform rather than redesigning captions per export.

## What Content Actually Needs Platform-Specific Editing (Not Just Resizing)

Some content needs more than a resize - pacing, hook length, and even the opening 2 seconds need to change per platform because attention curves differ. TikTok and Reels viewers decide to keep watching within the first 1-2 seconds; YouTube (even Shorts) tends to tolerate a slightly slower build.

- **TikTok**: front-load the payoff or hook in the first second; trends and sound sync matter more than production polish.
- **Instagram Reels**: similar hook speed to TikTok but slightly more tolerance for polished visuals and text-heavy openings.
- **YouTube Shorts**: can open a half-second slower since the audience is often already subscribed and more patient.
- **YouTube long-form**: can carry a 10-30 second intro without losing most viewers, assuming the topic was clear from the thumbnail and title.

This is the part that's easy to underestimate - resizing the frame is mechanical, but resizing the pacing without re-editing the actual cut is where most DIY multi-platform posting falls short.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use the same video file for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts without changes?**
A: The aspect ratio (9:16) is the same across all three, so the file will technically play fine everywhere. But safe zones for captions and UI elements differ slightly between them, so a caption placed safely for TikTok may get partially covered by Instagram's caption bar or YouTube's subscribe button.

**Q: What's the biggest mistake people make with cross-platform video sizing?**
A: Exporting once in 16:9 and uploading that same file to TikTok, Reels, and Shorts with black bars added top and bottom instead of reframing the shot for vertical. It's an instant signal that the content wasn't made for that platform.

**Q: Do I need different resolution for TikTok versus Instagram Reels?**
A: No, both use the same 1080x1920 vertical format at similar bitrate ranges. The difference is in safe zones and UI placement, not resolution or aspect ratio.

**Q: Should Instagram feed posts be vertical like Reels?**
A: No. Instagram feed posts typically perform better at 4:5 or 1:1 rather than full 9:16 vertical, since the feed grid and scroll behavior favor slightly shorter frames over full-vertical Reels-style video.
