## Why Manual Review Requests Fail

Business owners try review requests three ways: verbally at the point of service, a follow-up phone call, or a generic "please review us" email blast sent once a month. All three share the same weakness — they depend on human memory and bandwidth, which are the two things a busy local business has least of.

Verbal asks get forgotten under pressure. Follow-up calls compete with actual paying work. Monthly email blasts go out to a stale list and get buried in inboxes that already have 40 unread messages. None of these are systems — they're intentions.

| Verbal ask at job site | Low | Inconsistent | Low but easily skipped |
| Manual follow-up call | Very low | Low, drops off fast | High |
| Monthly email blast | Medium | Low, list fatigue | Medium |
| Automated SMS trigger (GHL) | High | Higher, sent while fresh | Near zero after setup |

The pattern is obvious once you see it laid out: automation isn't better because it's flashy, it's better because it removes the dependency on someone remembering to do it.

## How GHL Automates the Review Request

The mechanic is straightforward. Inside GHL, a workflow is built around a trigger — this could be a job status update, an invoice marked paid, a calendar appointment marked completed, or a tag applied manually by staff. Once triggered, the workflow sends a text message (and often a parallel email) with a short, direct message and a link to leave a Google review.

A typical sequence looks like this:

- **Trigger:** Job/appointment status changes to "Completed"
- **Delay:** Wait 15-30 minutes so the customer isn't messaged mid-service
- **Message 1 (SMS):** Short thank-you with direct Google review link
- **Wait 2-3 days:** If no review detected/link not clicked
- **Message 2 (Email or SMS):** Friendly reminder, same link
- **Optional:** Internal notification to owner if a low rating is flagged before posting

The direct Google review link matters more than most people realize. It should take the customer straight to the star-selection screen on the business's Google Business Profile — not to the homepage, not to a general "contact us" page. Every extra click costs response rate.

## Setting Up the Review Filter (Carefully)

A common tactic is to insert a short feedback question before sending customers to Google: "How was your experience?" with a 1-5 star selector inside the message itself. Customers who select 4-5 stars get routed to the public Google review link. Customers who select 1-3 stars get routed to a private form instead, so the business owner can address the issue directly.

This protects the public rating from an occasional bad day turning into a permanent 1-star review, and it gives the business a chance to fix the problem before it becomes public. That said, this tactic needs a caveat: using this filter to *systematically suppress* negative reviews from ever being visible anywhere, including preventing customers from posting organically if they choose to, crosses into territory Google's review policies frown on. The filter should route feedback, not silence customers who still want to post publicly.

| Rating Given | Route | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 4-5 stars | Public Google review link | Capture public social proof |
| 1-3 stars | Private feedback form | Resolve issue, prevent public blowup |
| No response | Follow-up reminder (1x) | Recover missed responses |

## Building the Workflow Step-by-Step

Most agencies setting up GHL reputation management for local business follow a similar build pattern. It doesn't need to be complex to work — in fact, simpler workflows tend to have fewer points of failure.

1. **Connect the Google Business Profile** integration inside GHL so review data and links sync properly.
2. **Create the trigger** — job completion, invoice paid, or a manual tag applied by staff at the point of service.
3. **Build the message template** — short, first-name personalized, one clear call to action, no more than 2-3 sentences.
4. **Add a delay** — sending immediately during active service work looks robotic and gets ignored.
5. **Add a follow-up branch** for non-responders after 48-72 hours.
6. **Add reporting** — a dashboard or notification so the owner can see review requests sent vs. reviews received.

The reporting step gets skipped constantly, and it's the one that proves the system is working. Without it, nobody knows if the workflow quietly broke three weeks ago.

## What to Put in the Actual Message

The message itself matters more than the automation logic behind it. A request that reads like a corporate form letter gets ignored. A request that reads like it came from an actual human who just helped you gets answered.

Keep the message short, name-specific, and free of exclamation-point sales energy. Something like: "Hi [First Name], thanks for choosing [Business Name] today. Would you mind leaving us a quick review? It really helps. [Link]" outperforms longer, more formal templates.

- Use the customer's first name, not "Valued Customer"
- Reference the specific service performed if possible ("thanks for letting us fix your AC today")
- One link, one ask — don't bundle a review request with a referral pitch or upsell
- Send from a recognizable business name, not a generic short code

## Measuring Whether It's Actually Working

A reputation automation system that isn't tracked is just a guess. The metrics that matter are review request send rate, review completion rate, and average rating trend over time. GHL's reporting can show requests sent and, depending on integration setup, clicks on the review link.

| Metric | What It Tells You |
|---|---|
| Requests sent per week | Whether the trigger is firing consistently |
| Review completion rate | Whether the message/timing is effective |
| Average star rating trend | Whether the filter and service quality are aligned |
| Time-to-review (send to post) | Whether delay timing is optimized |

If request volume is high but completion rate is low, the problem is usually the message or the delay timing, not the trigger. If request volume itself is low, the trigger setup is broken — check the integration first before touching the copy.

## FAQ

**Q: How does GHL automate Google reviews for local businesses?**
**A:** GHL triggers a text or email review request automatically when a job, appointment, or invoice is marked complete in the CRM. The message links directly to the Google review screen, removing the need for staff to remember to ask manually.

**Q: Can GHL filter out negative reviews before they post to Google?**
**A:** GHL workflows can route low-star feedback to a private form instead of the public Google link, letting the business address issues privately. This should be used to capture and resolve feedback, not to prevent customers who want to post negative reviews from doing so.

**Q: What triggers should I use to start a review request workflow?**
**A:** Common triggers include a job status change to "completed," an invoice marked paid, or a calendar appointment finished. Some businesses also use a manual tag applied by staff at the point of service completion.

**Q: How soon after service should the review request be sent?**
**A:** A short delay of 15-30 minutes after job completion typically works best — long enough that the customer isn't messaged mid-service, short enough that the experience is still fresh in their mind.
